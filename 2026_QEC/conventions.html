Topological codes (Module 3 and later)

Object Convention
Lattice Kitaev's: qubits on the edges of a square lattice; X-type checks on vertices (stars) Av=∏e∋vXeA_v=\prod_{e\ni v}X_eAv​=∏e∋v​Xe​; Z-type checks on plaquettes Bp=∏e∈∂pZeB_p=\prod_{e\in\partial p}Z_eBp​=∏e∈∂p​Ze​. Z errors are detected by vertex checks and form strings on the primal lattice with eee anyons at the ends; X errors are detected by plaquette checks and form strings on the dual lattice with mmm anyons at the ends. Zˉ\bar ZZˉ is a Z-string on a non-contractible primal cycle and Xˉ\bar XXˉ an X-string on a non-contractible dual cycle. A source that puts Z on vertices is translated in a crosswalk.
Boundaries Named by their truncated checks: an X-boundary is one along which the truncated checks (weight 3 unrotated, weight 2 rotated) are X-type; X-strings end there without a syndrome, so Xˉ\bar XXˉ runs between the two X-boundaries. Likewise the Z-boundary. Rough and smooth are geometric names for the unrotated lattice (rough: edges dangle; smooth: the lattice ends on complete plaquettes). With Kitaev's assignment a rough boundary has truncated Z-plaquettes and is the Z-boundary; a smooth boundary has truncated X-stars and is the X-boundary. Swapping the Pauli assignment swaps the names, which is why sources appear to disagree.
Counts Every count says what it includes. Rotated distance-ddd code: d2d^2d2 data, d2−1d^2-1d2−1 measure, 2d2−12d^2-12d2−1 in all. Unrotated: d2+(d−1)2d^2+(d-1)^2d2+(d−1)2 data, 2d(d−1)2d(d-1)2d(d−1) measure, (2d−1)2(2d-1)^2(2d−1)2 in all. Toric L×LL\times LL×L: 2L22L^22L2 data, 2L2−22L^2-22L2−2 independent checks.
